# Landing Page Audit Rubric

Use this rubric to score existing landing pages on conversion potential. Each of the 10 dimensions scores 1-5, for a total possible score of 50.

## How to Use This Rubric

1. Score each dimension honestly based on what's actually on the page, not what could be improved
2. For each score below 4, note the specific reason
3. After scoring, identify the 2-3 dimensions with the highest gap between current score and ideal (5). These are the highest-leverage fixes.
4. Prioritize fixes that address dimensions scoring 1-2 first. They're producing the biggest conversion drag.

## Scoring Dimensions

### 1. Headline Clarity

**5 — Excellent:** The offer is immediately clear. Specific, benefit-driven, readable in under 2 seconds.
**4 — Good:** Offer is clear but could be sharper or more specific.
**3 — Adequate:** Headline is workable but generic. Doesn't differentiate.
**2 — Poor:** Vague or jargon-heavy. Visitor has to read more to understand the offer.
**1 — Failing:** Says nothing useful ("Welcome!", "Innovative Solutions"). Or missing entirely.

### 2. Subheading Value

**5 — Excellent:** Communicates a specific value proposition that complements the headline. Tells visitors why this matters to them.
**4 — Good:** Adds value but could be more specific or compelling.
**3 — Adequate:** Provides context but doesn't add much beyond restating the headline.
**2 — Poor:** Generic, repeats the headline, or adds confusion.
**1 — Failing:** Missing, or actively undermines the headline.

### 3. Hero Visual Relevance

**5 — Excellent:** Image or video directly represents the offer. Visitor sees what they're getting.
**4 — Good:** Visual supports the offer, even if not a literal representation.
**3 — Adequate:** Visual is on-brand but doesn't strongly connect to the offer.
**2 — Poor:** Generic stock photo. Could appear on any page.
**1 — Failing:** Mismatched, distracting, or missing.

### 4. Value Proof (Bullets / Body Copy)

**5 — Excellent:** Specific, concrete bullets that prove value. Numbers, deliverables, outcomes.
**4 — Good:** Clear bullets but could be more specific.
**3 — Adequate:** Provides some value information but vague in places.
**2 — Poor:** Mostly generic claims. "Industry-leading," "comprehensive," "best-in-class."
**1 — Failing:** Missing, or so vague it provides no value information.

### 5. Form Design

**5 — Excellent:** Form length matches funnel stage. Fields are clear, single-column layout, labels and placeholders work together.
**4 — Good:** Form is solid with minor issues (one extra field, slightly unclear labels).
**3 — Adequate:** Form works but isn't optimized. Maybe too many fields or poor visual design.
**2 — Poor:** Significant friction. Too many fields for the offer, multi-column layout, unclear field purposes.
**1 — Failing:** Form is overwhelming, broken, or asks for inappropriate information for the offer stage.

### 6. CTA Button

**5 — Excellent:** Button copy is specific, action-oriented, benefit-clear. Visually prominent. Easy to find.
**4 — Good:** Button is clear with minor copy improvements possible.
**3 — Adequate:** Button copy is generic but acceptable ("Sign Up," "Get Started").
**2 — Poor:** Vague copy ("Submit," "Continue"), poor visual prominence.
**1 — Failing:** Multiple competing CTAs, hidden button, or confusing action.

### 7. Privacy & Trust

**5 — Excellent:** Privacy reassurance is clear, specific, and near the form. Builds confidence without legalese.
**4 — Good:** Privacy info present but could be more reassuring or better placed.
**3 — Adequate:** Generic privacy link present, no specific reassurance.
**2 — Poor:** Privacy info hidden or written in scary legalese.
**1 — Failing:** No privacy information visible. Visitors don't know what happens with their data.

### 8. Social Proof

**5 — Excellent:** Specific, credible social proof near the form. Real names, titles, photos, or recognizable logos with framing.
**4 — Good:** Solid social proof but could be more prominent or specific.
**3 — Adequate:** Some social proof present but generic or buried.
**2 — Poor:** Generic testimonials without attribution, or social proof only at the bottom of a long page.
**1 — Failing:** No social proof visible.

### 9. Distraction Removal

**5 — Excellent:** No external navigation. Single focused conversion path. Footer minimal (privacy + terms only).
**4 — Good:** Mostly distraction-free with minor issues (one or two extra links).
**3 — Adequate:** Some navigation removed but still has competing exit paths.
**2 — Poor:** Full site navigation present. Visitor can easily leave the page.
**1 — Failing:** Page treated like a standard website page. Multiple competing CTAs and exit paths.

### 10. Visual Hierarchy & Above-the-Fold Composition

**5 — Excellent:** Eye is naturally drawn to headline → value → form/CTA. All key elements above the fold on desktop and mobile.
**4 — Good:** Strong hierarchy with minor issues.
**3 — Adequate:** Hierarchy is present but unclear. Form might be below the fold.
**2 — Poor:** Visual hierarchy fights the conversion goal. Form is hard to find.
**1 — Failing:** No clear hierarchy. Visitor can't tell what to do or where to look.

## Total Score Interpretation

- **45-50:** Strong landing page. Optimization gains will be marginal. Focus on testing the highest-leverage elements (offer, headline, image).
- **35-44:** Solid foundation with clear improvement areas. Fix the 2-3 lowest-scoring dimensions for meaningful gains.
- **25-34:** Significant conversion drag. Multiple dimensions need work. Consider a rebuild rather than incremental fixes.
- **Below 25:** The page is actively hurting conversion. Rebuild from scratch using the anatomy and copywriting references.

## Highest-Leverage Fix Identification

After scoring, identify the top fixes by combining two factors:

1. **Score gap:** How far is the current score from 5? Bigger gap = more potential lift.
2. **Dimension weight:** Some dimensions affect conversion more than others. Roughly:
   - **Highest weight:** Headline, Hero Visual, Form Design, CTA Button
   - **High weight:** Subheading, Value Proof, Social Proof
   - **Medium weight:** Privacy, Distraction Removal, Visual Hierarchy

A score of 2 on Headline is more urgent than a score of 2 on Privacy. Prioritize accordingly.

## What's Working
