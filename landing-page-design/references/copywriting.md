# Landing Page Copywriting

Patterns, formulas, and examples for every major copy element on a landing page. Use this when writing or rewriting copy for any component.

## Headline Patterns

The headline must do one job: tell the visitor what they're getting and why it matters, in under 2 seconds of reading.

### Pattern 1: Specific Offer

State exactly what the visitor will get. Best for clear, well-defined offers.

- "Get the 2024 SaaS Pricing Benchmark Report"
- "Download 50 Email Templates That Closed Real Deals"
- "Reserve Your Seat at the Free Demand Gen Workshop"

### Pattern 2: Outcome-Focused

Lead with the result the visitor will get from the offer.

- "Cut Your CAC in Half With These 7 Frameworks"
- "Write Better Cold Emails in 10 Minutes"
- "Find $50K in Hidden Revenue Inside Your Funnel"

### Pattern 3: Question Hook

Pose the question the visitor is already asking.

- "Why Aren't Your Landing Pages Converting?"
- "Is Your Pricing Page Costing You Customers?"

### Pattern 4: Direct Command

Tell the visitor what to do, what's next.

- "Sign Up for Your Free Account."
- "Start Your 14-Day Trial."
- "Book a Demo With Our Team."

### Pattern 5: Bold Claim

Make a specific, provable claim. Best when you have the data to back it up.

- "The Only Marketing Course Built by 7-Figure Founders"
- "Trusted by 12,000+ B2B SaaS Companies"

### Headline Anti-Patterns

- **Vague welcome:** "Welcome!" / "Hi There!" / "Hello"
- **Brand-first:** "Acme Corp's New Solution" (visitor doesn't care about you yet)
- **Jargon stack:** "Synergistic AI-Powered Solutions for Enterprise Innovation"
- **Cleverness without clarity:** "The Sky's the Limit" (limit on what?)
- **Feature dumping:** "Now With 200+ Integrations and Real-Time Reporting"

## Subheading Patterns

The subheading expands on the headline by communicating the value proposition. It should answer: "Why should I care?"

### Pattern 1: Headline + Subhead = Complete Offer

The headline says what. The subhead says why.

- Headline: "The Conversion Collection"
  Subhead: "Generate Even More Leads From Your Blog"

- Headline: "Money Matters"
  Subhead: "Your Guide for Financial Security"

### Pattern 2: Headline + Subhead = Action + Outcome

The headline tells them what to do. The subhead tells them what they'll get.

- Headline: "Watch a Demo of HubSpot's Software"
  Subhead: "See how you can increase your company's revenue with our all-in-one marketing platform."

- Headline: "Get a Free Strategic Analysis"
  Subhead: "Our team will review your SEO and content marketing and send you a custom playbook."

### Pattern 3: Headline + Subhead = Hook + Proof

The headline hooks attention. The subhead provides credibility.

- Headline: "Cut Your Sales Cycle in Half"
  Subhead: "The exact playbook used by 200+ B2B SaaS companies, distilled into 30 pages."

### Subhead Length

Aim for one sentence. Two if needed. Never more than three lines on desktop.

## Value Statement Bullets

The 3-5 bullets under the subheading that prove value with specifics.

### Strong Bullet Patterns

**Pattern: Specific deliverable**
- "12 plug-and-play email templates"
- "A 47-point pre-launch checklist"
- "Real campaign data from 50+ B2B companies"

**Pattern: Specific outcome**
- "Reduce time-to-first-meeting by 40%"
- "Build your first sequence in under an hour"
- "Identify your highest-converting traffic source"

**Pattern: What you'll learn**
- "Why most cold emails fail (and the 3 things that fix them)"
- "How to price for value, not effort"
- "When to discount and when to walk away"

### Weak Bullet Patterns

- "Industry-leading insights" (vague)
- "Best practices for success" (says nothing)
- "Comprehensive coverage" (no specifics)
- "Cutting-edge strategies" (jargon)
- "Everything you need to know" (overpromise)

### Bullet Formatting

- Start with a verb or concrete noun, not a soft intro phrase
- 1 line ideal, 2 lines max
- Lead with the most compelling bullet first
- Use specific numbers when possible (not "many," "lots," "several")

## Form Copy

Every word in or around the form affects conversion.

### Form Headline

The mini-headline above the form, separate from the main page headline.

**Strong form headlines:**
- "Get Your Free Copy"
- "Download Now"
- "Reserve Your Spot"
- "Start Your Free Trial"

**Weak form headlines:**
- "Sign Up" (sign up for what?)
- "Contact Us" (no value communicated)
- "Form" (this happens more than you'd think)

### Field Labels

- Use clear, single-word labels when possible: "Email," "Name," "Company"
- Avoid corporate jargon: "Business Email Address" → "Work Email"
- For optional fields, mark them with "(optional)" rather than marking required fields with asterisks (cleaner visual)

### Placeholder Text

- Use placeholders for examples, not labels: `you@company.com`, not `Email Address`
- Don't replace labels with placeholders. Both is better than one.

### Privacy Reassurance Copy

Place directly under the submit button or below the last field.

**Strong reassurance:**
- "We'll never share your email. Unsubscribe anytime."
- "No spam, ever. Just the guide and occasional updates."
- "Your info is safe. See our [privacy policy]."

**Weak reassurance:**
- "We respect your privacy." (says nothing specific)
- "By submitting, you agree to our terms..." (legalese, scary)

## Button Copy (CTA)

The most tested copy element on a landing page. Small changes can produce large conversion lifts.

### The "Submit" Trap

Never use "Submit." It's vague, motivationless, and tells visitors nothing about what happens. Replace it on every page you encounter it.

### Strong CTA Patterns

**Pattern: Get + [specific thing]**
- "Get the Free Guide"
- "Get My Pricing Calculator"
- "Get Instant Access"

**Pattern: Download + [specific thing]**
- "Download the Ebook"
- "Download Your Free Trial"
- "Download the Templates"

**Pattern: Start + [specific action]**
- "Start My Free Trial"
- "Start the Course"
- "Start Building"

**Pattern: Reserve / Claim**
- "Reserve My Spot"
- "Claim My Discount"
- "Reserve a Demo"

**Pattern: Send Me**
- "Send Me the Guide"
- "Send Me the Templates"
- "Send Me Updates"

### First-Person vs Second-Person

First-person ("Get My Guide") often outperforms second-person ("Get Your Guide") because it mentally puts the visitor in the action. But test it. Audiences differ.

### CTA Length

- 2-5 words is the sweet spot
- "Get the Guide" beats "Get Your Free Comprehensive Marketing Guide Now"
- Verb + noun is the strongest structure

### Multi-Step CTA Hint

For high-friction offers (demos, trials), reassure with secondary text below the button:
- Button: "Start My Free Trial"
  Below: "No credit card required. Cancel anytime."

## Social Proof Copy

How to write the words that go around testimonials, logos, and stats.

### Testimonial Format

Strong testimonials follow this pattern:
- **Quote:** Specific outcome or transformation
- **Name + Title + Company:** Real attribution
- **Photo:** Builds trust

**Strong example:**
> "We doubled our trial-to-paid conversion in 90 days using this exact playbook."
> — Sarah Chen, VP Marketing, [Company]

**Weak example:**
> "Great product, highly recommend!"
> — John S.

### Logo Bar Framing

Don't just dump logos. Frame them:
- "Trusted by teams at:"
- "Used by 12,000+ companies including:"
- "As seen in:"

### Stat-Based Social Proof

Specific numbers beat vague claims:
- "Join 47,000 marketers" (specific)
- "Join thousands of marketers" (vague)

- "4.8 stars from 1,200 verified reviews" (specific, sourced)
- "Highly rated by users" (vague)

## Body Copy Style

For longer landing pages with explanatory sections:

### Sentence Length

Mix short and medium sentences. Avoid run-ons. Read your copy out loud. If you run out of breath, the sentence is too long.

### Paragraph Length

2-3 sentences max for landing page body copy. Walls of text kill conversion. White space is your friend.

### Voice

- Active voice over passive: "We send you the guide" beats "The guide is sent to you"
- Concrete over abstract: "Cut your CAC by 30%" beats "Improve marketing efficiency"
- Direct over indirect: "Click below" beats "Visitors may wish to consider clicking"

### Reading Level

Aim for 6th-8th grade reading level. This isn't dumbing down. It's respecting the reader's time. Hemingway Editor or similar tools help check this.

## Tone Calibration

The right tone depends on the audience and offer. A few archetypes:

**Professional / B2B SaaS:** Direct, benefit-focused, light on humor, heavy on specificity
- "Cut your sales cycle in half. The playbook is free."

**Friendly / Consumer:** Warmer, conversational, contractions welcome
- "Want better skin? Here's how. (Free, no spam, promise.)"

**Authoritative / Expert:** Confident, data-backed, light on adjectives
- "The 2024 SaaS Pricing Benchmark Report. 1,247 companies analyzed."

**Playful / Bold:** Distinctive, opinionated, willing to take a stance
- "Most landing pages are bad. Here's how to make yours great."

Match the tone to the audience, not your own preference.
