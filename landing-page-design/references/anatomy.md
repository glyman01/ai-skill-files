# Landing Page Anatomy

The 10 essential components of an optimized landing page. Use this as the structural blueprint when building or auditing a page.

## 1. No Navigation

**What:** Remove or hide top and side navigation bars, footer link clusters, and any cross-page navigation.

**Why:** Navigation creates exit paths. Every link is a potential leak in your conversion funnel. Removing it keeps visitors focused on the single desired action.

**Best practice:** Keep the logo (linked to home is fine, but consider unlinked) and minimal legal links in the footer (privacy, terms). That's it.

## 2. Headline

**What:** The first text visitors see. Usually large, above the fold, top of the page.

**Why it matters:** Whether visitors stay or leave often depends entirely on the headline. It must communicate the offer immediately.

**Strong headlines:**
- State the offer specifically
- Lead with the benefit, not the feature
- Are short enough to read in under 2 seconds

**Examples that work:**
- "Sign up for your free account."
- "Work Smarter with Evernote: Online Workshop"
- "Get our tips straight to your inbox and become a better manager."

**Examples that fail:**
- "Welcome!" (says nothing)
- "Innovative Solutions for Modern Businesses" (vague, jargon-heavy)
- "The Future of Marketing is Here" (no specific offer)

## 3. Subheading

**What:** Smaller text directly below the headline that expands on the offer.

**Why it matters:** This is where you communicate the value proposition. The headline tells visitors what they get. The subheading tells them why it matters.

**Strong subheading patterns:**
- "Headline: [What]. Subhead: [Why it's valuable to you]"
- "Headline: [Benefit]. Subhead: [Specific outcome or proof point]"

**Examples that work:**
- Headline: "The Conversion Collection" / Subhead: "Generate Even More Leads From Your Blog"
- Headline: "Money Matters" / Subhead: "Your Guide for Financial Security"
- Headline: "Watch a demo of HubSpot's software" / Subhead: "See how you can increase your company's revenue with our all-in-one marketing platform."

## 4. Value Statements

**What:** A short list (3-5 items) of specific things the visitor will get, learn, or be able to do.

**Why it matters:** For visitors who aren't sold by the headline alone, value statements anticipate questions and provide concrete proof of value. They make the offer feel real and specific.

**Format:**
- Bulleted list, not paragraph
- Each bullet starts with a verb or concrete benefit
- 1-2 lines per bullet, max
- Specific, not vague

**Strong value bullets:**
- "Templates for 12 different email sequences you can copy/paste"
- "A pricing calculator that works for SaaS, services, and ecommerce"
- "Real campaign data from 50+ B2B companies"

**Weak value bullets:**
- "Industry-leading insights" (vague)
- "Best practices for success" (says nothing)
- "Comprehensive coverage of the topic" (no specifics)

## 5. Image (Hero Visual)

**What:** A relevant visual that supports the offer. Cover image of an ebook, screenshot of a product, headshot for a webinar, etc.

**Why it matters:** Most people process images before words. A compelling image grabs attention faster than any headline. It also makes the offer feel tangible.

**Strong image choices:**
- Mockup of the deliverable (ebook cover, dashboard screenshot, certificate, etc.)
- Photo of the speaker/expert if it's a webinar or course
- Visual representation of the discount or offer
- Branded illustration that matches the offer's theme

**Weak image choices:**
- Generic stock photos of people in offices smiling at laptops
- Abstract shapes that don't communicate anything
- Photos that contradict the offer (formal photo for a casual offer, or vice versa)

## 6. Video (Optional but Powerful)

**What:** Embedded video, often with autoplay or a compelling thumbnail.

**Why it matters:** Adding video to a landing page can lift conversion rates substantially. Videos show rather than tell, demand less effort from users, and keep them on the page longer.

**Implementation best practices:**
- Use a thumbnail that signals what the video contains (animated 5-second clips work well)
- Include closed captions (most users watch with sound off, especially on mobile)
- Place CTAs inside the video at peak engagement moments, not just at the end
- Consider an in-video form for an additional conversion path
- Use video heatmaps to identify where viewers drop off, then place CTAs just before those points

**Technical note:** Don't upload video files directly to the page. Use a hosting platform with embed codes to avoid hurting page load speed.

## 7. Lead Capture Form

**What:** The form where visitors hand over their information in exchange for the offer. Usually 2-7 fields.

**Why it matters:** This is the conversion point. Everything else exists to drive visitors to fill out this form.

**The fundamental tradeoff:**

| Form Length | Lead Quantity | Lead Quality |
|-------------|---------------|--------------|
| Shorter (2-3 fields) | More leads | Lower quality |
| Longer (5-7+ fields) | Fewer leads | Higher quality |

**Decision framework:**
- **Top-of-funnel offers** (ebooks, checklists, blog subscriptions): Email only, or email + first name
- **Middle-of-funnel** (case studies, webinars, ROI tools): Add company, role, or company size
- **Bottom-of-funnel** (demos, free trials, consultations): Ask for whatever sales needs to qualify (phone, team size, current solution, etc.)

**Form placement:**
- Always above the fold on desktop, ideally on mobile too
- If the page is long-form, repeat the form (or at least a CTA that scrolls to the form) further down

**Field design:**
- Use single-column layout (multi-column hurts completion rates)
- Mark required fields clearly
- Use placeholder text or labels that match what the field expects
- Avoid dropdowns when possible. Radio buttons or autocomplete are better.

## 8. Privacy Reassurance

**What:** A short note, link, or both, explaining how the visitor's information will be used.

**Why it matters:** Lack of privacy reassurance discourages form completion. Visitors fear their info being sold or spammed.

**Implementation options:**
- One-line text directly under or near the submit button
- Link to full privacy policy
- Both (recommended)

**Strong reassurance copy:**
- "We'll never share your email. Unsubscribe anytime."
- "Your information is safe. See our [privacy policy]."
- "No spam, ever. Just the guide and occasional updates you can opt out of."

**Critical:** Only promise what you'll actually deliver. Promising "no marketing emails" then sending marketing emails destroys trust permanently.

## 9. Button Copy (CTA)

**What:** The text on the submit button. The visitor's final cue to act.

**Why it matters:** Button copy is the last thing visitors read before deciding to convert or bounce. Generic copy reduces conversions. Specific copy increases them.

**Anti-pattern:** "Submit." Vague, no motivation, no clarity about what happens next. Replace it everywhere.

**Strong button copy patterns:**
- "Download [the specific thing]" — "Download Your Ebook," "Download the Guide"
- "Get [the specific thing]" — "Get Your Free Trial," "Get the Discount"
- "Start [the specific action]" — "Start My Free Trial," "Start the Course"
- "Send Me [the thing]" — "Send Me the Checklist," "Send Me Updates"
- "Reserve [the thing]" — "Reserve My Spot," "Reserve a Demo"

**First-person ("Get My Guide") often outperforms second-person ("Get Your Guide"),** but test it.

## 10. Social Proof

**What:** Evidence from third parties that the offer or company is trustworthy and valuable.

**Why it matters:** Visitors trust other customers more than they trust your claims about yourself. Social proof transfers credibility.

**Forms of social proof, in rough order of effectiveness for landing pages:**

1. **Specific testimonials with names, titles, and photos** — quotes from real people about the specific outcome they got
2. **Case study snippets** — "How [company] used this to [result]"
3. **Logos of recognizable customers or media features** — "As seen in [Forbes, TechCrunch, etc.]"
4. **Aggregate numbers** — "Used by 50,000 marketers" or "4.8 stars from 1,200 reviews"
5. **Embedded social media posts** — tweets or LinkedIn posts praising the offer

**Placement:** Near the form, or just before the form on a long-form page. Social proof at the bottom of a long page is wasted on visitors who already left.

**Anti-pattern:** Generic testimonials without names, titles, or photos ("Great product! - John S."). These hurt credibility instead of helping.

## Above-the-Fold Priorities

The first screen view (above the fold) should contain at minimum:
- Headline
- Subheading
- Hero image or video
- Form OR a prominent CTA that scrolls to the form
- One piece of social proof (logos, rating, or testimonial)

If any of these are below the fold, conversion rate suffers.

## Mobile Considerations

Mobile traffic often exceeds desktop. The landing page must work on mobile, which means:
- Form must be reachable without excessive scrolling
- Buttons must be large enough to tap (44px minimum)
- Text must be readable without zooming
- Video must autoplay muted (with captions) or have a clear play prompt
- Page load time must be under 3 seconds (ideally under 2)
